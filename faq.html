Helpful hints from Tara

Sourdough questions, answered

The questions we hear most, from feeding your starter to slicing your first loaf.

What will I need to bake at home?

A digital kitchen scale, a medium bowl (glass is best), a spatula, a proofing basket or loaf tin, a scoring blade, a jar for your starter, a bench scraper, parchment paper and a Dutch oven or other oven-safe pot with a lid. See Tara's Sourdough Toolkit for the ones she uses.

What flour and salt should I use?

Unbleached all-purpose white flour (Tara uses Costco Organic), filtered water, and Himalayan or sea salt rather than iodized table salt. Rice flour is handy for dusting your proofing basket.

How often should I feed my starter?

Feed it every time before and after you use it. If you're not baking, try to feed it once or twice a week, and at least every 2 to 4 weeks, to keep it strong and healthy.

Where should I keep my starter?

If you bake every day it can live on the counter. Otherwise keep it in the fridge with a lid on, but not at the very back where it might freeze. Let it come to room temperature before feeding.

How do I know my starter is ready to use?

When it "doubles and bubbles". It should have risen to about twice its size, with lots of bubbles and a slightly domed top, usually 4 to 10 hours after feeding. It should smell warm, milky and tangy, and feel light and stringy. You can also do the float test: a spoonful of active starter floats in water.

What is discard?

Discard is the extra, unfed starter you remove before feeding. You can throw it away or keep it in the fridge for recipes like pancakes, tortillas, cookies and muffins. It won't give the same rise as an active starter.

There's grey liquid on top of my starter. Is it ruined?

No. That liquid is called hooch, and it means your starter is hungry. Pour it off or stir it in at the next feeding. Only throw your starter away if you see mold.

My starter isn't rising. What should I do?

Remove some, then feed it again and give it time. A starter that has been in the fridge for a while may need two or three feedings to get strong again. Warmer water and a warmer spot help it rise faster.

I'm going away for a few weeks. What do I do with my starter?

Feed it, cover it with a lid and put it in the fridge. When you're back, let it come to room temperature and feed it. There will probably be hooch on top, which is fine as long as there's no mold.

How hot should the oven be, and for how long?

Preheat to 450°F with your cast iron Dutch oven inside for 45 to 60 minutes. Bake 30 minutes with the lid on, then 8 to 15 minutes with the lid off. Don't preheat non-cast-iron pans, just the oven.

How do I know my bread is done?

The inside of the loaf should read 205 to 210°F on a thermometer.

The bottom of my bread keeps burning. Any tips?

Put a baking tray on the rack underneath your Dutch oven. It shields the bottom of the loaf.

Why is my bread gummy inside?

It was probably cut while still warm. Let your loaf cool on a rack for at least 2 hours before slicing. We know it's hard!

Can I pour leftover dough or starter down the sink?

Please don't. It hardens and can clog your pipes. Put it in the garbage or compost instead.

Is it suitable for people with celiac disease or a wheat allergy?

Unfortunately not. Classes use wheat flour.

My first loaf didn't turn out. Now what?

Try again, and don't get discouraged. Every bake teaches you something. And whatever happens, always bake the bread!
